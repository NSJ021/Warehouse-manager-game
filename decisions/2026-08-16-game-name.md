# The game is called Nice Little Earner

- **Date:** 2026-08-16
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Deciders:** NJ

## Context

"Warehouse Manager" was a working title that was never chosen so much as inherited from the pitch. Three findings retired it.

**It collides.** `Warehouse Manager Simulator` already exists on Steam, alongside `Warehouse Simulator`, `Warehouse Inc.`, `Warehouse and Logistics Simulator` and the 2026 co-op rival `Pack and Ship: Warehouse Simulator`. Steam search would have put us in a queue behind our own competitors, competing for our own name.

**It promises the wrong shelf.** The word "Manager" sells an optimisation sim — precisely the £16.75 lane the price ADR rejected. A name that sets the expectation of a spreadsheet is actively working against a game whose hook is physics comedy and fraud.

**It describes the setting rather than the game.** The warehouse is no longer the differentiator; every competitor has one. The differentiator is the dilemma and the tone. A name should carry the thing competitors cannot copy, and the setting is the thing they already have.

The naming search ran across four registers: warehouse jargon (*Shrinkage*, *Two Man Lift*), the deflection (*Came In Like That*, *That'll Buff Out*), the legal disclaimer (*Sold As Is*, *No Warranty*), and character (*Chancers*, *On The Fiddle*). Steam collision checks were run on roughly sixty candidates.

## Decision

**The game is called `Nice Little Earner`.**

British idiom, popularised by Arthur Daley in *Minder*, meaning a profitable bit of business that is not entirely above board. It states the crew's motive — we are here for the money and we are not fussy how — before anyone has explained a single mechanic.

Clear on Steam search: no exact or near collision.

The name commits the game to a **character-led identity** rather than the deadpan-institutional register the genre defaults to (*Lethal Company*, *Content Warning*, *Schedule I*). The joke is who you are, not what the paperwork says.

## Consequences

**Easier:** it is unique in search and immediately establishes tone, which is the one asset none of the earnest warehouse sims can replicate. It gives the art direction, store copy and trailer voice a clear brief. It makes the parked sales-counter proposal feel native rather than bolted on — "earner" implies actively making money in ways you would rather not itemise.

**Harder:** it is UK-weighted. A US player, and the US is the largest market, will not parse "earner" as idiom. The mitigation is that it still *sounds* bent, and the genre's own precedents (*R.E.P.O.*, *PEAK*) prove a name can set tone by sound without being understood literally. Capsule art and store copy carry the meaning.

It also raises the floor on tone: a game called *Nice Little Earner* has to be funny and characterful, and it will be judged against that promise. Flat, earnest execution reads worse under this name than under a neutral one.

**Follow-ups before the Steam page exists:**
- A proper trademark search. The phrase is common English and appears as a song title (4 or 5 Magicians) and in film and television writing, none of which is a games-category conflict — but a search is required, not optional, and this ADR does not substitute for one.
- Re-run the Steam collision check immediately before reserving the page.
- Decide whether the repo, Godot project folder and GitHub remote are renamed to match, or left as-is. Renaming touches paths outside the repository and is a separate operational decision.

## Alternatives considered

**`Sold As Is`** — the strongest of the disclaimer register. Universal, deadpan, and it means "the damage is your problem now" in three words. Rejected because that register is the genre's default and therefore the crowded one; it would have read as competent rather than distinctive.

**`No Warranty`** — clean, universal, semantically correct as the sign above your own counter. Rejected as colder and less distinctive than *Sold As Is*, which already lost.

**`Chancers`** — one word, punchy, names the players rather than the job. The runner-up, and the strongest option if the name ever needs to travel further than this one does.

**`Shrinkage`** — the actual industry term for stock that vanished unexplained, which is exactly the crime. Rejected as too oblique on a capsule, and carrying an unhelpful second connotation.

**`No Questions Asked`** — conceptually the best match for the pillar of any candidate found. Rejected on an exact Steam collision.
